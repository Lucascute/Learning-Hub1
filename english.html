📖 English 10 — Term 1

These notes cover the English 10 lessons from Lesson 1 to Lesson 10.

The topics focus on drama, literary contexts, short film writing and production, characterization, and literary concepts used in short films.

🎭 Lesson 1: Structural Context of Drama as a Film

The structural context refers to how the different parts of a drama are organized and connected to create a complete story.

Important Elements

  • Characters – the people or figures involved in the story.
  • Setting – the time and place where the story happens.
  • Plot – the sequence of events in the story.
  • Conflict – the main struggle or problem faced by the characters.
  • Dialogue – the spoken words of characters.
  • Theme – the central idea or message of the drama.

Basic Plot Structure

  1. Exposition – introduces the characters, setting, and situation.
  2. Rising Action – problems and conflicts begin to develop.
  3. Climax – the most intense or important point of the story.
  4. Falling Action – events begin to move toward the conclusion.
  5. Resolution – the conflict is resolved or the story comes to an ending.
Remember:

A film uses visual elements such as scenes, camera shots, actions, and dialogue to show the structure of a drama.

📚 Lesson 2: Understanding Literary Context

Literary context refers to the circumstances surrounding a literary work that help readers understand its meaning.

Types of Context

Historical Context

The historical events and conditions that existed when the work was written or takes place.

Cultural Context

The beliefs, traditions, values, customs, and practices of a society.

Social Context

The social conditions and relationships that influence the characters and story.

Political Context

Political situations, systems, or events that influence the literary work.

Why is context important?

Understanding context helps readers understand why characters behave in certain ways and why certain events happen in the story.

🧠 Lesson 3: Frayer's Method

The Frayer Method is a strategy used to understand and remember vocabulary and concepts.

Part Meaning
Definition The meaning of the word or concept.
Characteristics Important qualities or features.
Examples Things that demonstrate the concept.
Non-examples Things that do not demonstrate the concept.
Example: Conflict

Definition: A struggle between opposing forces.

Characteristics: Creates tension and problems in a story.

Example: Two characters disagree about an important decision.

Non-example: Two characters peacefully agreeing.

🔎 Lesson 4: Evaluating Literary Texts Through the Psychological Context of a Drama

The psychological context focuses on the thoughts, feelings, motivations, emotions, and behavior of characters.

What to Examine

  • Thoughts – What is the character thinking?
  • Emotions – What does the character feel?
  • Motivation – Why does the character act that way?
  • Behavior – How does the character respond to situations?
  • Relationships – How do other characters affect the character?

Questions to Ask

  1. What is the character feeling?
  2. What motivates the character?
  3. What experiences influence the character?
  4. How does the character change?
  5. How do emotions affect the plot?
Quick idea:

Psychological analysis asks: "What is happening inside the character?"

🎬 Lesson 5: Conceptualizing and Writing a Short Film Script

A short film script is a written plan for a film. It describes the characters, scenes, actions, dialogue, and events.

Important Parts of a Script

  • Title – name of the film.
  • Characters – people involved in the story.
  • Setting – where and when the scene happens.
  • Scene – a specific part of the film.
  • Action – what the characters do.
  • Dialogue – what the characters say.

Writing Process

1

Brainstorm ideas – think of possible story ideas.

2

Choose a concept – select the strongest idea.

3

Develop characters – create believable characters.

4

Develop the plot – organize the events.

5

Write the script – combine scenes, actions, and dialogue.

✏️ Lesson 6: Revising, Editing, and Publishing a Short Film Script

Revising

Revising means improving the content, structure, and overall quality of the script.

  • Improve the plot.
  • Make scenes clearer.
  • Improve character development.
  • Strengthen dialogue.
  • Remove unnecessary parts.

Editing

Editing focuses on correcting errors in the script.

  • Grammar
  • Spelling
  • Punctuation
  • Capitalization
  • Sentence structure

Publishing

Publishing means preparing the final version of the script for presentation or production.

Easy way to remember:

Revising = Improve
Editing = Correct
Publishing = Finalize and Present

🎥 Lesson 7: Basics of Filming and Film Production

Film production is the process of planning, recording, and creating a finished film.

Three Main Stages

Stage Description
Pre-production Planning, scripting, casting, preparing locations, and organizing equipment.
Production The actual filming or recording of scenes.
Post-production Editing video and audio and completing the final film.

Basic Filming Elements

  • Camera
  • Lighting
  • Sound
  • Actors
  • Location
  • Props
  • Costumes

🎥 Lesson 8: Dos and Don'ts in Filming a Short Film

✅ DOs

  • Plan your scenes before filming.
  • Keep the camera steady.
  • Use proper lighting.
  • Record clear audio.
  • Check the camera angle.
  • Practice scenes before recording.
  • Keep the filming location organized.
  • Review footage after recording.

❌ DON'Ts

  • Don't record with extremely poor lighting.
  • Don't allow unnecessary background noise.
  • Don't shake the camera unnecessarily.
  • Don't ignore the script.
  • Don't forget to check the audio.
  • Don't record without planning the shot.
  • Don't leave important equipment unattended.
Remember:

Good preparation makes filming easier and helps produce better-quality scenes.

👤 Lesson 9: STEAL Format for Characterization

STEAL is a method for analyzing and describing characterization by examining how a character is presented in a story.

S — Speech

What does the character say? What does the character's way of speaking tell us about them?

T — Thoughts

What does the character think? What do their thoughts reveal about them?

E — Effect on Others

How do other characters react to or respond to this character?

A — Actions

What does the character do? What do their actions reveal?

L — Looks

What does the character look like? Consider appearance, clothing, and physical characteristics.

Quick Example:

If a character constantly helps others, their Actions can show that they are caring or generous.

🎞️ Lesson 10: Organic Unity, Binary Opposition, Co-text, and Collocation in Short Films

🌱 Organic Unity

Organic unity means that the different parts of a literary work or film work together as one complete and meaningful whole.

Characters, setting, plot, dialogue, visuals, and theme should support one another.

⚖️ Binary Opposition

Binary opposition refers to two contrasting ideas, characters, forces, or concepts.

Examples include:

  • Good vs. Evil
  • Light vs. Darkness
  • Freedom vs. Oppression
  • Rich vs. Poor
  • Love vs. Hate

📝 Co-text

Co-text refers to the words, sentences, scenes, or surrounding elements that help us understand the meaning of something within a text or film.

The meaning of a line or action can change depending on what happens around it.

🔗 Collocation

Collocation refers to words that commonly occur together or have a natural relationship in language.

Examples:

  • make a decision
  • strong argument
  • heavy rain
  • take responsibility
Quick Review:

Organic Unity → Parts work together as one.

Binary Opposition → Contrasting ideas.

Co-text → Surrounding text or elements that help explain meaning.

Collocation → Words that commonly go together.
