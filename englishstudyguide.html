🎬 Lesson 1 — Structural Context of Drama as a Film

What is structural context?

Structural context refers to how the different parts of a literary or dramatic work are organized and connected.

Important Elements

  • Plot – the sequence of events in the story.
  • Characters – people or figures involved in the story.
  • Setting – where and when the story takes place.
  • Conflict – the main problem or struggle in the story.
  • Theme – the central idea or message.
💡 Remember: When analyzing a film, look at how the plot, characters, setting, conflict, and theme work together.

📖 Lesson 2 — Understanding Literary Context

What is literary context?

Literary context refers to the circumstances, background, culture, history, and ideas surrounding a literary work.

Things to Consider

  • Historical background
  • Cultural background
  • Social conditions
  • Author's background
  • Time period
  • Values and beliefs
Example:

Understanding the historical period when a story was written can help explain why characters behave in certain ways and why certain themes appear.

🧠 Lesson 3 — Frayer's Method

What is Frayer's Method?

The Frayer Model is a graphic organizer used to understand and remember vocabulary and concepts.

The Four Parts

  • Definition – meaning of the word.
  • Characteristics – important qualities or features.
  • Examples – things that represent the concept.
  • Non-examples – things that do not represent the concept.
⭐ Study Tip: When learning a difficult literary term, use all four parts instead of memorizing only the definition.

🔎 Lesson 4 — Psychological Context of a Drama

What is psychological context?

Psychological context focuses on the thoughts, emotions, motivations, experiences, and behavior of characters.

Questions to Ask

  • What is the character feeling?
  • What motivates the character?
  • Why does the character behave that way?
  • What experiences influenced the character?
  • How does the character change?
💡 Remember: Psychological analysis looks beyond what a character does and asks why the character does it.

✍️ Lesson 5 — Conceptualizing and Writing a Short Film Script

What is a short film script?

A short film script is a written plan that describes the story, characters, dialogue, actions, and scenes of a short film.

Basic Parts

  • Title
  • Characters
  • Setting
  • Scenes
  • Actions
  • Dialogue
  • Conflict
  • Ending
Basic Structure:

Beginning → Introduce characters and situation

Middle → Conflict develops

Ending → Conflict is resolved or reaches a conclusion

📝 Lesson 6 — Revising, Editing and Publishing a Short Film Script

Why revise and edit?

Revising and editing improve the clarity, organization, grammar, and overall quality of a script.

Revision

Revision focuses on improving the ideas, structure, characters, scenes, and overall story.

Editing

Editing focuses on grammar, spelling, punctuation, word choice, and formatting.

Publishing

Publishing is preparing the final work so it can be presented or shared with an audience.

💡 Remember: Revision = ideas and organization
Editing = language and correctness
Publishing = final presentation

🎥 Lesson 7 — Basics of Filming and Film Production

What is film production?

Film production is the process of planning, recording, editing, and producing a film.

Main Stages

  • Pre-production – planning, scripting, casting, locations, and preparation.
  • Production – actual filming.
  • Post-production – editing, sound, effects, and finalizing the film.
🎥 Remember: Pre-production → Production → Post-production

⚠️ Lesson 8 — Dos and Don'ts in Filming Short Films

Good filming practices

Proper planning and teamwork help make filming organized, safe, and effective.

✅ DO

  • Plan the scenes before filming.
  • Check the camera and audio.
  • Use proper lighting.
  • Keep the camera stable.
  • Communicate with your team.
  • Respect filming locations.
  • Record multiple takes when necessary.

❌ DON'T

  • Don't ignore background noise.
  • Don't film without permission when permission is required.
  • Don't intentionally damage equipment.
  • Don't block important scenes with unnecessary objects.
  • Don't forget to check your recordings.

👤 Lesson 9 — STEAL Format for Characterization

What is STEAL?

STEAL is a method for analyzing how an author reveals the personality and characteristics of a character.

STEAL Means:

  • S — Speech
    What the character says and how they speak.
  • T — Thoughts
    What the character thinks and feels.
  • E — Effect on Others
    How other characters react to the character.
  • A — Actions
    What the character does.
  • L — Looks
    The character's appearance or physical description.
⭐ Easy way to remember:

Speech — Thoughts — Effect — Actions — Looks

🧩 Lesson 10 — Organic Unity, Binary Opposition, Co-text and Collocation

Organic Unity

Organic unity means that the different parts of a literary work are connected and contribute to the overall meaning of the work.

Binary Opposition

Binary opposition refers to contrasting pairs of ideas, characters, or concepts.

Examples:

Good ↔ Evil
Light ↔ Darkness
Love ↔ Hate
Rich ↔ Poor
Co-text

Co-text refers to the words and sentences surrounding a particular word or expression that help determine its meaning.

Collocation

Collocation refers to words that commonly appear together in a language.

Examples:

make a decision
heavy rain
strong coffee
💡 Quick Review:

Organic Unity → parts work together
Binary Opposition → contrasting ideas
Co-text → surrounding words
Collocation → words that commonly go together

⭐ English Study Tips

  1. Understand literary terms. Don't memorize definitions without knowing how they are used.
  2. Practice analyzing characters. Use STEAL when studying characterization.
  3. Look for evidence. Support your interpretation with details from the text or film.
  4. Know the differences. Make sure you can distinguish context, co-text, collocation, and binary opposition.
  5. Practice writing. When writing a script, focus on clear scenes, dialogue, conflict, and character development.
  6. Review film production. Remember the three major stages: pre-production, production, and post-production.
  7. Use your own examples. Creating examples makes literary concepts easier to remember.
