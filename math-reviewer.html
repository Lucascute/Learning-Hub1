Week 1: Law of Sines and Ambiguous Case

📖 Law of Sines

The Law of Sines relates the sides of a triangle to the sines of their opposite angles.

📐 Formula

a / sin(A) = b / sin(B) = c / sin(C)

📝 Example

Problem:

Suppose a = 10, A = 30°, and B = 45°. Find side b.

Using the Law of Sines:

10 / sin(30°) = b / sin(45°)

Solve for b:

b = 10 × sin(45°) / sin(30°)
Answer: b ≈ 14.14

⚠️ Ambiguous Case

The ambiguous case occurs when the Law of Sines is used with SSA information. Depending on the given measurements, there may be two possible triangles, one possible triangle, or no possible triangle.

📌 Remember

  • Use the Law of Sines when you know an angle and its opposite side.
  • Check whether the given information can produce one or two triangles.
  • Always check whether the calculated angle is possible for the triangle.

Week 2: Trigonometric Functions

📖 Basic Trigonometric Ratios

The three basic trigonometric ratios are sine, cosine, and tangent.

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite / adjacent

📌 Remember

A useful way to remember the ratios is SOH-CAH-TOA.

↑ Back to Top

Week 3: Trigonometric Identities

📖 What is an Identity?

A trigonometric identity is an equation involving trigonometric functions that is true for all values where both sides are defined.

📐 Important Identity

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

📌 Remember

Trigonometric identities can be used to simplify expressions and solve equations.

📖 Solving Trigonometric Equations

Trigonometric equations contain unknown angles inside trigonometric functions.

📝 Example

Example:

Solve for θ:

sin θ = 1/2

One principal angle is 30°. Other solutions may occur depending on the required interval.

Week 5: Graphs of Trigonometric Functions

📖 Trigonometric Graphs

Trigonometric functions can be represented using graphs. Important features include amplitude, period, and midline.

📌 Important Terms

  • Amplitude: The distance from the midline to a maximum or minimum.
  • Period: The length of one complete cycle.
  • Midline: The horizontal center line of the graph.

Week 7: Sequences

📖 Arithmetic Sequence

An arithmetic sequence is a sequence in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant.

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

📖 Geometric Sequence

A geometric sequence is a sequence in which each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a constant ratio.

aₙ = a₁rⁿ⁻¹

Week 8: Series

📖 What is a Series?

A series is the sum of the terms of a sequence.

📐 Arithmetic Series

Sₙ = n/2 [2a₁ + (n − 1)d]

📐 Geometric Series

Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r)

Week 9: Probability

📖 Basic Probability

Probability measures how likely an event is to happen.

P(E) = favorable outcomes / total possible outcomes

📝 Example

Problem:

A fair die has six possible outcomes. The probability of rolling a 3 is:

P(3) = 1 / 6
Answer: 1/6

Week 10: Statistics

📖 Measures of Central Tendency

Measures of central tendency describe the center of a set of data.

  • Mean: The sum of all values divided by the number of values.
  • Median: The middle value when the data is arranged in order.
  • Mode: The value that occurs most frequently.

📐 Mean Formula

Mean = Sum of all values / Number of values

📌 Remember

Arrange data properly before finding the median and check the frequency of values when finding the mode.
