📐 Law of Sines

What is it?

The Law of Sines is used to find unknown sides or angles of a triangle when you know enough information about the triangle.

a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C

When to Use It

  • When you know two angles and one side.
  • When you know two sides and an opposite angle.
  • When the triangle is not necessarily a right triangle.
💡 Remember: The side and its opposite angle must be paired correctly.

🔺 Ambiguous Case

What is it?

The ambiguous case occurs when using the Law of Sines with two sides and a non-included angle (SSA).

Possible Results

  • No triangle
  • One triangle
  • Two possible triangles
💡 Study Tip: Always check whether the given measurements can form a triangle before assuming there is only one answer.

📏 Law of Cosines

What is it?

The Law of Cosines is useful for finding an unknown side or angle when the triangle does not fit the easiest Law of Sines cases.

c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

When to Use It

  • When you know two sides and the included angle (SAS).
  • When you know all three sides (SSS).
💡 Remember: The angle in the formula must be opposite the side represented by c.

🧭 Bearings

What are bearings?

Bearings describe direction using angles measured clockwise from North.

Important Rules

  • North = 000°
  • East = 090°
  • South = 180°
  • West = 270°
💡 Remember: Bearings are normally written using three digits, such as 045°, 120°, or 315°.

🔄 Transformations

What are transformations?

Transformations change the position, size, or orientation of a figure.

Four Main Transformations

  • Translation – moves a figure.
  • Reflection – flips a figure over a line.
  • Rotation – turns a figure around a point.
  • Dilation – changes the size of a figure.
💡 Study Tip: Practice identifying the transformation before trying to calculate the new coordinates.

📊 Quadratic Inequalities

What are they?

Quadratic inequalities involve expressions such as x² and use inequality symbols: <, >, ≤, or ≥.

Common Method

  1. Move everything to one side.
  2. Factor the quadratic if possible.
  3. Find the critical values.
  4. Test intervals.
  5. Choose the interval that satisfies the inequality.
Example:

x² − 5x + 6 > 0

Factor: (x − 2)(x − 3) > 0

Critical values: x = 2 and x = 3

➗ Absolute Value Equations

What is absolute value?

Absolute value represents the distance of a number from zero on the number line.

|x| = a → x = a or x = −a
Example:

|x| = 5

Therefore:

x = 5 or x = −5

💡 Remember: Absolute value is never negative.
↑ Back to Top

📈 Absolute Value Inequalities

Important Rules

Absolute value inequalities can be rewritten as compound inequalities.

|x| < a → −a < x < a
|x| > a → x < −a or x > a
💡 Easy Way to Remember:

"Less than" → BETWEEN
"Greater than" → OUTSIDE

📊 Measures of Position

What are measures of position?

Measures of position describe where a particular value is located within a dataset.

Important Measures

  • Quartiles
  • Percentiles
  • Deciles
  • Percentile Rank
💡 Study Tip: Always arrange the data from smallest to largest before finding measures of position.
↑ Back to Top

📌 Percentile Rank

What does it mean?

Percentile rank tells you the percentage of scores that are at or below a particular score.

💡 Example: If your score is at the 80th percentile, you performed as well as or better than approximately 80% of the group.

⭐ Mathematics Study Tips

  1. Understand the formula. Don't just memorize it. Know what each variable represents.
  2. Write down the given information. This makes word problems much easier.
  3. Draw a diagram. This is especially useful for triangles, bearings, and geometry problems.
  4. Show your solution. Don't skip steps even if you can solve the problem mentally.
  5. Check your answer. Make sure the answer makes sense.
  6. Practice difficult problems. Don't only practice the easy examples.
  7. Review mistakes. Your mistakes can show you exactly what you need to study.
