📘 Science 10 — Quick Reviewer

A short reviewer covering the major Science 10 topics from Week 1 to Week 10.

⚗️ Week 1: Chemical Reactions, Acids, Bases, and Salts

A chemical reaction occurs when substances change into new substances with different properties.

Acids

  • Usually have a pH below 7.
  • Can taste sour.
  • Examples: vinegar and lemon juice.

Bases

  • Usually have a pH above 7.
  • Often feel slippery.
  • Examples: soap and baking soda.

Salts

Salts are ionic compounds that can form from reactions between acids and bases.

Remember: Acid + Base → Salt + Water

🧪 Week 2: Types of Chemical Reactions

  • Synthesis – two or more substances combine.
  • Decomposition – one compound breaks apart.
  • Single Replacement – one element replaces another.
  • Double Replacement – parts of two compounds exchange.
  • Combustion – a substance reacts with oxygen and releases energy.

🏷️ Week 2.1: Naming Compounds

Chemical compounds are named using rules based on the elements and ions that make them up.

Ionic Compounds

Usually consist of a metal and a nonmetal.

Example: NaCl = Sodium Chloride

Covalent Compounds

Usually consist of nonmetals and may use prefixes such as mono-, di-, tri-, and tetra-.

⚖️ Weeks 3–5: Chemical Equations

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using chemical formulas and symbols.

Parts of an Equation

  • Reactants – substances that start the reaction.
  • Products – substances produced by the reaction.
  • Arrow – shows the direction of the reaction.
Example:

H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

Balancing Chemical Equations

Chemical equations are balanced by changing the coefficients, not the chemical formulas.

Balanced example:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Why Balance Equations?

Equations must be balanced because matter is conserved during a chemical reaction.

Remember: The number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of the equation.

🔥 Weeks 6–7: How Chemical Reactions Occur

Chemical reactions occur when particles collide with enough energy and in the proper orientation.

Reaction Rate

The rate of reaction describes how quickly reactants are changed into products.

Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

  • Temperature – higher temperature usually increases reaction rate.
  • Concentration – higher concentration can increase collisions.
  • Surface Area – greater surface area can increase reaction rate.
  • Catalyst – speeds up a reaction without being consumed.
  • Nature of Reactants – different substances react at different rates.

🫀 Week 8: Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the ability of an organism to maintain stable internal conditions despite changes in the environment.

Examples

  • Maintaining body temperature.
  • Maintaining blood sugar levels.
  • Maintaining water balance.
  • Maintaining proper pH.
Key idea: Homeostasis keeps the internal environment of the body relatively stable.

🧍 Week 9: Homeostasis in the Human Body

The human body uses different systems and feedback mechanisms to maintain internal balance.

Examples

  • Sweating helps cool the body.
  • Shivering helps produce heat.
  • Insulin helps regulate blood glucose.
  • The kidneys help regulate water and waste.
Negative Feedback: A change triggers a response that helps return the body toward its normal condition.

🧬 Week 10: Mechanism of Evolution

Evolution refers to changes in inherited characteristics of populations over generations.

Natural Selection

Organisms with traits that help them survive and reproduce are more likely to pass those traits to future generations.

Week 10.1: Principles of Evolution

  • Variation exists among organisms.
  • Some variations are inherited.
  • Organisms compete for limited resources.
  • Individuals with advantageous traits may survive and reproduce more successfully.
  • Populations can change over generations.

Week 10.2: Evidence Supporting Evolution

  • Fossils – show organisms that lived in the past.
  • Comparative Anatomy – compares body structures of organisms.
  • Embryology – compares similarities in early development.
  • Biogeography – studies the distribution of organisms.
  • Molecular Evidence – compares DNA and other biological molecules.
Remember: Multiple lines of evidence support the idea that living organisms have changed over time.
