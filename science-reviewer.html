🔬 Lesson 1: Scientific Investigation

Scientific investigation is a systematic way of studying questions and problems using evidence and observations.

Important Steps

  1. Identify the problem.
  2. Ask a question.
  3. Form a hypothesis.
  4. Conduct an experiment.
  5. Collect and analyze data.
  6. Draw a conclusion.
Remember: A hypothesis is a testable prediction or possible explanation.

Variables

  • Independent Variable – the variable being changed.
  • Dependent Variable – the variable being measured.
  • Controlled Variables – factors kept constant.

🌎 Lesson 2: Earth's Structure

Earth is made up of several layers with different compositions and properties.

Main Layers

  • Crust – thin outer layer of Earth.
  • Mantle – thick layer beneath the crust.
  • Outer Core – liquid layer mainly composed of iron and nickel.
  • Inner Core – solid, extremely hot central part of Earth.
Key Idea: Temperature and pressure generally increase as depth inside Earth increases.

🌋 Lesson 3: Volcanoes

A volcano is an opening in Earth's crust through which molten rock, gases, and other materials can escape.

Important Terms

  • Magma – molten rock beneath Earth's surface.
  • Lava – magma that reaches Earth's surface.
  • Crater – opening at or near the top of a volcano.
  • Ash – tiny solid particles produced during eruptions.

Types of Volcanoes

  • Shield volcano
  • Cinder cone
  • Composite volcano
Remember: Volcanoes can be classified based on their shape, activity, and eruption history.

🌍 Lesson 4: Earthquakes and Seismic Waves

An earthquake is the sudden movement or shaking of Earth's crust caused by the release of energy.

Important Terms

  • Focus – point inside Earth where an earthquake begins.
  • Epicenter – point on Earth's surface directly above the focus.
  • Fault – fracture or break in Earth's crust where movement occurs.

Types of Seismic Waves

  • P-waves – fastest seismic waves.
  • S-waves – slower than P-waves and cannot travel through liquids.
  • Surface waves – travel along Earth's surface.

⚡ Lesson 5: Force and Motion

Force is a push or pull that can change the motion of an object.

Types of Forces

  • Gravity
  • Friction
  • Magnetic force
  • Applied force
  • Normal force

Newton's Second Law

F = m × a

Where:

  • F = force
  • m = mass
  • a = acceleration
Remember: Increasing force generally increases acceleration when mass remains constant.

🧬 Lesson 6: Cell Division

Cell division allows organisms to grow, repair damaged tissues, and reproduce.

Mitosis

Mitosis produces two genetically similar daughter cells.

  • Prophase
  • Metaphase
  • Anaphase
  • Telophase
  • Cytokinesis

Meiosis

Meiosis produces sex cells and results in cells with half the original chromosome number.

Quick Difference: Mitosis is mainly associated with growth and repair, while meiosis produces gametes.

🧬 Lesson 7: Genetics and Heredity

Genetics is the study of heredity and variation in organisms.

Important Terms

  • Gene – unit of heredity.
  • Allele – different form of a gene.
  • Genotype – genetic makeup of an organism.
  • Phenotype – observable characteristics.
  • Dominant – allele that can mask a recessive allele.
  • Recessive – allele expressed when the appropriate conditions are met.
Remember: Genotype refers to genes, while phenotype refers to observable traits.

🌱 Lesson 8: Evolution

Evolution refers to changes in inherited characteristics of populations over generations.

Natural Selection

Natural selection occurs when organisms with advantageous traits are more likely to survive and reproduce.

Important Concepts

  • Variation
  • Adaptation
  • Natural selection
  • Inheritance
  • Survival and reproduction
Key Idea: Individuals do not evolve because they "need" to. Populations change over generations through inherited variation and selection.

🌿 Lesson 9: Ecosystems

An ecosystem consists of living organisms interacting with one another and with their physical environment.

Biotic and Abiotic Factors

  • Biotic – living components such as plants, animals, and microorganisms.
  • Abiotic – nonliving components such as water, sunlight, soil, and temperature.

Levels of Organization

  1. Organism
  2. Population
  3. Community
  4. Ecosystem
  5. Biosphere

🌎 Lesson 10: Energy Flow in Ecosystems

Energy flows through an ecosystem from one organism to another.

Food Chain

A food chain shows the transfer of energy between organisms.

Sun → Producers → Consumers → Decomposers

Important Roles

  • Producers – make their own food, usually through photosynthesis.
  • Consumers – obtain energy by eating other organisms.
  • Decomposers – break down dead organisms and waste.
Remember: Energy decreases as it moves through higher trophic levels.

⭐ Quick Science Review

  • 🔬 A hypothesis is a testable prediction.
  • 🌎 Earth has a crust, mantle, outer core, and inner core.
  • 🌋 Magma is beneath Earth's surface; lava reaches the surface.
  • 🌍 The epicenter is directly above the earthquake's focus.
  • ⚡ Force can change the motion of an object.
  • 🧬 Mitosis is important for growth and repair.
  • 🧬 Genes carry hereditary information.
  • 🌱 Natural selection can lead to adaptations in populations.
  • 🌿 Ecosystems contain biotic and abiotic factors.
  • ☀️ Energy enters most ecosystems through the Sun.

💡 Science Study Tips

  1. Understand, don't just memorize. Try to explain every concept using your own words.
  2. Memorize important vocabulary. Science questions often depend on understanding key terms.
  3. Use diagrams. Draw Earth's layers, cell division stages, food chains, and other processes.
  4. Compare similar concepts. For example, compare mitosis vs. meiosis and genotype vs. phenotype.
  5. Practice explaining "why." Don't only memorize the answer. Understand why it happens.
  6. Review a little every day. Ten to fifteen minutes of review can be better than cramming everything the night before.
