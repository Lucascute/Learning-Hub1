🔬 Science 10 — Term 1

Short and easy-to-review notes covering chemical reactions, chemical equations, reaction rates, homeostasis, and evolution.

🧪 Week 1: Chemical Reactions, Acids, Bases, and Salts

A chemical reaction occurs when substances change into new substances with different properties.

Acids

  • Usually have a sour taste.
  • Have a pH below 7.
  • Examples: vinegar and lemon juice.

Bases

  • Usually feel slippery.
  • Have a pH above 7.
  • Examples: soap and baking soda.

Salts

Salts are ionic compounds that can form from acid-base reactions.

Remember: Acid → pH below 7 | Neutral → pH 7 | Base → pH above 7

⚗️ Week 2: Types of Chemical Reaction

Chemical reactions can be classified according to how substances combine or change.

  • Synthesis – two or more substances combine to form one product.
  • Decomposition – one compound breaks into simpler substances.
  • Single Replacement – one element replaces another.
  • Double Replacement – two compounds exchange ions.
  • Combustion – a substance reacts with oxygen and releases energy.
Quick clue:
Synthesis = combine
Decomposition = break apart

🏷️ Week 2.1: Naming Compounds

Chemical compounds are named using rules based on the elements or ions they contain.

Ionic Compounds

Usually contain a metal and a nonmetal. The metal name comes first, followed by the nonmetal with an -ide ending.

NaCl → Sodium chloride
MgO → Magnesium oxide

Covalent Compounds

Usually contain nonmetals and use prefixes to show the number of atoms.

CO₂ → Carbon dioxide

⚖️ Weeks 3–5: Chemical Equations

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using chemical formulas and symbols.

Parts of a Chemical Equation

  • Reactants – substances present before the reaction.
  • Products – substances formed after the reaction.
  • Arrow (→) – shows the direction of the reaction.

Balancing Chemical Equations

Equations are balanced by placing coefficients in front of chemical formulas. 

H₂ + O₂ → H₂O 
 
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Why is balancing important?

It follows the Law of Conservation of Mass: matter is not created or destroyed during a chemical reaction.

Important: Change coefficients, not subscripts, when balancing equations.

⏱️ Weeks 6–7: How Chemical Reactions Occur

Chemical reactions occur when particles collide with enough energy and in the proper orientation.

Reaction Rate

The rate of reaction is how quickly reactants are converted into products.

Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

  • Temperature – higher temperature usually increases reaction rate.
  • Concentration – higher concentration can increase collisions.
  • Surface Area – greater surface area can increase reaction rate.
  • Catalyst – speeds up a reaction without being consumed.
  • Nature of Reactants – different substances react at different rates.
Quick idea: More effective collisions usually mean a faster reaction.

🏠 Week 8: Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the body's ability to maintain a stable internal environment despite changes outside the body.

Examples

  • Maintaining body temperature.
  • Controlling blood sugar.
  • Maintaining water balance.
  • Regulating blood pressure.
Remember: Homeostasis = maintaining internal balance.

❤️ Week 9: Homeostasis — Maintaining Balance Inside the Human Body

The human body uses different organs and systems to keep internal conditions within a normal range.

Examples

  • Temperature regulation – sweating helps cool the body.
  • Blood sugar – hormones help regulate glucose levels.
  • Water balance – the kidneys help control water and salts.
Example: When body temperature rises, sweating helps remove heat and return the body toward its normal temperature.

🧬 Week 10: Mechanism of Evolution

Evolution is the change in inherited characteristics of populations over generations.

Important Mechanisms

  • Natural Selection – organisms with favorable traits are more likely to survive and reproduce.
  • Mutation – changes in DNA that can create new variations.
  • Gene Flow – movement of genes between populations.
  • Genetic Drift – random changes in allele frequencies.

🌱 Week 10.1: Principles of Evolution

Evolution is based on variation, heredity, and differences in survival and reproduction.

  • Individuals in a population have variations.
  • Some variations can be inherited.
  • Organisms compete for limited resources.
  • Individuals with favorable traits may reproduce more successfully.
  • Over many generations, populations can change.
Natural selection: Helpful inherited traits can become more common in a population over generations.

🔎 Week 10.2: Evidence That Supports Evolution

Scientists use different types of evidence to understand evolutionary relationships.

  • Fossils – provide evidence of organisms from the past.
  • Comparative Anatomy – compares body structures of organisms.
  • Embryology – compares similarities in developing organisms.
  • DNA Evidence – similarities in DNA can show common ancestry.
  • Biogeography – studies the geographic distribution of organisms.
Key idea: Multiple lines of evidence support the scientific explanation of evolution.
