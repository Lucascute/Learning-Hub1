📘 Science 10 Study Guide

This study guide gives a short review of the main Science 10 topics from Week 1 to Week 10.

Study Focus: Understand the important concepts, remember the key terms, and practice applying them to examples.

🧪 Week 1: Chemical Reactions, Acids, Bases, and Salts

A chemical reaction happens when substances change into new substances with different properties.

Acids

  • Usually have a sour taste.
  • Have a pH below 7.
  • Examples include vinegar and lemon juice.

Bases

  • Often feel slippery.
  • Have a pH above 7.
  • Examples include soap and baking soda.

Salts

Salts are compounds that can form when an acid reacts with a base.

Remember: Acid + Base → Salt + Water

⚗️ Week 2: Types of Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions can be classified according to how substances combine or change.

  • Synthesis → substances combine to form a new substance.
  • Decomposition → one compound breaks into simpler substances.
  • Single Replacement → one element replaces another element.
  • Double Replacement → parts of two compounds exchange places.
  • Combustion → a substance reacts with oxygen and releases energy.

🧾 Week 2.1: Naming Compounds

Chemical compounds are named using rules that identify the elements or ions present.

Important Ideas

  • Identify the elements in the compound.
  • Determine whether the compound is ionic or molecular.
  • Use the appropriate naming rules.
  • Remember common prefixes for molecular compounds.
Common prefixes: mono, di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa, hepta, octa, nona, deca

⚖️ Weeks 3 to 5: Chemical Equations

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using chemical formulas and symbols.

Parts of an Equation

  • Reactants are the substances that start the reaction.
  • Products are the substances formed by the reaction.
  • The arrow shows the direction of the reaction.

Balancing Chemical Equations

A chemical equation is balanced when the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides.

Why balance equations?

Chemical equations must be balanced because of the Law of Conservation of Mass. Matter is not created or destroyed during a chemical reaction.

Tip: Change coefficients to balance an equation. Do not change the subscripts of chemical formulas.

⏱️ Weeks 6 to 7: How Chemical Reactions Occur

Chemical reactions occur when particles collide with enough energy and with the proper orientation.

Reaction Rate

Reaction rate describes how quickly reactants are changed into products.

Factors Affecting Reaction Rate

  • Temperature → higher temperature can increase reaction rate.
  • Concentration → more particles can cause more collisions.
  • Surface Area → greater surface area can increase collisions.
  • Catalyst → can speed up a reaction without being consumed.
  • Nature of Reactants → different substances react at different rates.

🏠 Week 8: Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the ability of an organism to maintain stable internal conditions despite changes in the environment.

Examples

  • Maintaining body temperature.
  • Controlling blood sugar levels.
  • Maintaining proper water balance.
Remember: Homeostasis means maintaining internal balance.

❤️ Week 9: Homeostasis in the Human Body

The human body uses different systems and mechanisms to keep its internal environment stable.

Examples

  • Sweating helps cool the body.
  • Shivering helps produce body heat.
  • Insulin helps regulate blood glucose.
  • The kidneys help regulate water and waste.
Key Idea: The body continuously responds to changes to keep internal conditions within a suitable range.

🧬 Week 10: Mechanism of Evolution

Evolution is the change in inherited characteristics of populations over generations.

Important Mechanisms

  • Natural selection
  • Mutation
  • Genetic variation
  • Gene flow
  • Genetic drift

🌱 Week 10.1: Principles of Evolution

  • Populations contain variation.
  • Some variations can be inherited.
  • Organisms compete for limited resources.
  • Individuals with advantageous traits may survive and reproduce more successfully.
  • Over many generations, populations can change.
Natural Selection: Organisms with traits that help them survive and reproduce are more likely to pass those traits to future generations.

🔎 Week 10.2: Evidence Supporting Evolution

Scientists use different types of evidence to support the theory of evolution.

  • Fossils show organisms that lived in the past.
  • Comparative Anatomy compares body structures of organisms.
  • Embryology compares the development of organisms.
  • Biogeography studies the distribution of organisms.
  • Molecular Evidence compares DNA and other biological molecules.
Key Idea: Different lines of evidence support the idea that organisms have changed and diversified over time.

⭐ Final Science 10 Review

  • Chemical reactions produce new substances.
  • Acids have a pH below 7 and bases have a pH above 7.
  • Chemical reactions have different types.
  • Chemical compounds follow naming rules.
  • Chemical equations must be balanced.
  • Reaction rates are affected by several factors.
  • Homeostasis maintains internal balance.
  • Evolution describes changes in populations over generations.
  • Natural selection is an important mechanism of evolution.
  • Fossils, anatomy, embryology, biogeography, and molecular evidence support evolution.
Study Tip: Focus on understanding the ideas instead of only memorizing definitions. Practice balancing equations and explaining concepts in your own words.
