🧬 Lesson 1 — DNA and Heredity

What is DNA?

DNA or deoxyribonucleic acid is the molecule that contains the genetic instructions used by living organisms.

Important Terms

  • DNA – carries genetic information.
  • Gene – a segment of DNA that contains information for a particular trait.
  • Chromosome – a structure made of DNA and proteins that contains genes.
  • Heredity – the passing of traits from parents to offspring.
💡 Remember: Genes are found on chromosomes, and chromosomes contain DNA.

🧬 Lesson 2 — Genetics

What is genetics?

Genetics is the study of heredity and how traits are passed from parents to offspring.

Important Terms

  • Allele – an alternative form of a gene.
  • Dominant – an allele that can be expressed when present.
  • Recessive – an allele that is generally expressed only when two copies are present.
  • Genotype – the genetic makeup of an organism.
  • Phenotype – the observable characteristics of an organism.
Example:

If T represents a dominant allele and t represents a recessive allele:

TT = homozygous dominant
Tt = heterozygous
tt = homozygous recessive

🧫 Lesson 3 — Cell Division

What is cell division?

Cell division is the process by which a cell divides to produce new cells.

Mitosis

Mitosis produces two genetically similar daughter cells and is important for growth and repair.

Meiosis

Meiosis produces reproductive cells and results in cells with half the usual number of chromosomes.

💡 Remember: Mitosis → growth and repair
Meiosis → production of reproductive cells

🌱 Lesson 4 — Evolution

What is evolution?

Evolution refers to changes in inherited characteristics of populations over generations.

Natural Selection

Natural selection is a process in which organisms with traits that help them survive and reproduce are more likely to pass those traits to future generations.

💡 Key Idea: Individuals do not evolve because they "need" to. Populations change over generations as inherited traits become more or less common.

🌍 Lesson 5 — Biodiversity

What is biodiversity?

Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms in a particular area or on Earth.

Levels of Biodiversity

  • Genetic diversity
  • Species diversity
  • Ecosystem diversity
🌱 Remember: Biodiversity is important because ecosystems depend on interactions among many different organisms.

⚛️ Lesson 6 — Atomic Structure

What is an atom?

An atom is the basic unit of an element that retains the chemical properties of that element.

Subatomic Particles

  • Proton – positively charged.
  • Neutron – has no electrical charge.
  • Electron – negatively charged.
Atomic Number = Number of Protons
💡 Remember: Protons and neutrons are found in the nucleus, while electrons occupy regions around the nucleus.

🧪 Lesson 7 — Chemical Reactions

What is a chemical reaction?

A chemical reaction is a process in which substances are transformed into new substances.

Important Terms

  • Reactants – substances present at the beginning of a reaction.
  • Products – substances formed by the reaction.
⚖️ Law of Conservation of Mass: Matter is not created or destroyed in a chemical reaction. The atoms are rearranged.

⚡ Lesson 8 — Electricity

What is electricity?

Electricity involves the movement or presence of electric charge.

Important Concepts

  • Current – rate of flow of electric charge.
  • Voltage – potential difference that drives current.
  • Resistance – opposition to the flow of electric current.
V = IR

Where:

  • V = voltage
  • I = current
  • R = resistance

🌊 Lesson 9 — Waves

What is a wave?

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another.

Important Terms

  • Wavelength – distance between corresponding points on consecutive waves.
  • Frequency – number of complete waves passing a point per unit of time.
  • Amplitude – maximum displacement from the equilibrium position.
v = fλ

Wave speed = frequency × wavelength

🌎 Lesson 10 — Earth and Space

Earth and the Solar System

Earth is part of the Solar System, which includes the Sun and the objects that orbit it.

Important Concepts

  • Rotation – Earth's spinning on its axis.
  • Revolution – Earth's movement around the Sun.
  • Gravity – force of attraction between objects with mass.
  • Orbit – the path an object follows around another object.
🌎 Remember: Earth's rotation is associated with day and night, while its revolution around the Sun is related to the yearly cycle.

⭐ Science Study Tips

  1. Understand before memorizing. Try to explain a concept in your own words.
  2. Learn the vocabulary. Science exams often use important technical terms.
  3. Draw diagrams. Use diagrams for cells, atoms, waves, and other processes whenever possible.
  4. Connect concepts. Ask yourself how one scientific idea relates to another.
  5. Practice calculations. Don't just memorize formulas such as V = IR or v = fλ.
  6. Review mistakes. Write down concepts you got wrong and review them.
  7. Teach the topic. Try explaining the lesson to someone else. If you can explain it clearly, you probably understand it well.
